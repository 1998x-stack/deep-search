# Research methodology

## Stage 0 — scope and premise

Restate the central question as falsifiable subclaims. Record jurisdiction/geography, as-of date, temporal coverage, definitions, stakeholders and exclusions. A claim framed as an allegation must not be upgraded to an established fact without evidence.

## Stage 1 — discovery

Explore terminology and official source landscapes before optimizing narrow queries. Search original language, translations, alternative spellings, older company names and historical statute names. Use separate searches for mechanism, quantitative extent and counterevidence. Log every query with a timestamp, provider and result IDs.

## Stage 2 — document-level evidence

Prefer original judgments, filings, datasets, annual reports, peer-reviewed papers, product documentation, source code, interview recordings and primary testimony; assess what each can establish. Secondary coverage is useful for discovery and context, not necessarily a second independent confirmation.

## Stage 3 — hypotheses and contradiction

Create explicit alternatives: claim is true, partially true, false, time-limited, jurisdiction-specific, definition-dependent, or unsupported. Seek disconfirming evidence. Trace sensational numerical figures to the earliest accessible origin; inspect denominators and measurement periods.

## Stage 4 — research reflection

After each round write a brief ledger: answered subquestions, evidence gaps, contradictions, the one query most likely to change the conclusion, and remaining budget. Stop when new documents no longer change confidence in material claims or when resource limits are reached. Be explicit about unsearched avenues.

## Stage 5 — synthesis

Sort by strength of evidence, not narrative interest. Distinguish FACT (supported record), INFERENCE (reasoned from records), HYPOTHESIS (unverified explanation), and UNKNOWN. Cite each consequential assertion and note the date of validity. Include strongest counterarguments and limitations.

## Reproducibility minimum

Store plan, query log, source URLs, retrieval date, source text excerpts, exact claim text, polarity, reviewer decision, unresolved conflicts and final report. The CLI validates structural integrity, not source interpretation.
