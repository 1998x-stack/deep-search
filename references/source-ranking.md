# Source assessment rubric

Rank a source according to its suitability **for a particular claim** rather than a universal publisher score.

1. **Directness:** original document/data/testimony vs coverage of coverage.
2. **Authenticity:** verified provenance, stable official repository, author, integrity.
3. **Temporal validity:** publication date, event date, revision date and current applicability.
4. **Methodology:** definitions, denominators, sampling, replication and disclosed uncertainty.
5. **Independence:** different original collection or analysis—not a syndication cluster.
6. **Incentives:** financial, institutional and legal interests; do not automatically disqualify interested sources.
7. **Scope fit:** geography, population, system version or jurisdiction matches the researched claim.

A primary source can be authoritative about an organization's own statement but insufficient to prove independent effects of that statement. A judgment establishes adjudicated facts within its procedural context, not every interpretation about causality. Avoid arbitrary numerical confidence scores without calibrated data.
